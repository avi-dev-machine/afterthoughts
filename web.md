# PROJECT PROMPT — BUILD THE AFTERTHOUGHTS WEBSITE

## Project Overview

Create a complete, responsive editorial website for my personal technology and ideas publication:

# AFTERTHOUGHTS.

**Tagline:** A collection of thoughts on AI, technology and everything that comes next.

AFTERTHOUGHTS is a personal writing series exploring artificial intelligence, emerging technologies, human intelligence, computing, future possibilities and the questions surrounding technological progress.

This should feel like an independent digital magazine or a thoughtfully designed personal journal.

It should NOT look like:
- A generic technology blog.
- A corporate AI startup website.
- A SaaS landing page.
- A standard portfolio.
- A template-based article listing.

The website should have its own editorial identity.

The first chapter must be researched, written, compiled and published as a complete article before the website is considered finished.

---

# 1. BRAND AND VISUAL IDENTITY

Website name: AFTERTHOUGHTS.

Create a minimal, sophisticated and editorial design language.

### Design direction
- Warm off-white / ivory background.
- Deep charcoal typography.
- Muted grey secondary text.
- Restrained accent colour.
- Elegant serif typography for large editorial headlines.
- Clean sans-serif typography for body text and navigation.
- Generous whitespace.
- Fine borders and subtle separators.
- Carefully balanced typography and proportions.

The overall feeling should be a combination of an independent literary journal and a modern technology magazine.

Avoid:
- Excessive gradients.
- Neon colours.
- Glassmorphism.
- Unnecessary animations.
- Oversized rounded cards.
- Generic AI imagery.
- Excessive icons.
- Childish illustrations.

Use subtle motion only where it improves the reading experience.

# 3. CHAPTER 01 — RESEARCH, WRITE AND PUBLISH

This is the most important requirement.

Before completing the website, research and compile the following article:

**AI Should Be Our Calculator, Not Our Replacement**

Subtitle:

*Rethinking the relationship between human intelligence and artificial intelligence.*

The article should be a research-backed personal technology feature, not an academic paper.

Use the previously established Chapter 01 editorial brief.

### Core subject

Explore whether AI should replace human thinking or extend human capabilities.

Use the calculator analogy as the starting point.

Discuss:
- The history and significance of calculators as tools.
- Intelligence augmentation.
- Automation versus augmentation.
- Human-AI collaboration.
- Cognitive offloading.
- Automation bias.
- AI in education, software development, scientific research and agriculture.
- The limits of comparing generative AI with calculators.

### Technical content

Include meaningful, accessible mathematics.

Develop 2–3 simple mathematical explanations around:
- Time efficiency.
- Accuracy and error.
- Human-AI complementarity.

Define variables, show calculations and explain the practical implications.

Use hypothetical examples only when clearly labelled.

### Historical and research references

Naturally introduce the work of relevant thinkers, such as:
- Douglas Engelbart.
- J. C. R. Licklider.
- Alan Turing.
- Vannevar Bush.
- Erik Brynjolfsson.
- Daniel Kahneman.

Verify their original work, publications and claims.

Include credible external references and inline citations.

### Visual content

Create approximately 4–6 original visual elements:
- Calculator versus AI analogy.
- Human versus AI versus collaborative capability comparison.
- Efficiency and quality graph.
- Human-AI collaboration workflow.
- Cognitive dependence diagram.
- One real-world application illustration.

Use actual data where available. Clearly label hypothetical data and conceptual diagrams.

### Writing style

The article must feel:
- Personal.
- Curious.
- Conversational.
- Technically informed.
- Visually interesting.
- Thought-provoking.

Use short paragraphs, interesting section headings and occasional highlighted statements.

Target 1,800–2,500 words.

Avoid academic-paper formatting, generic AI introductions, excessive bullet points and exaggerated claims.

The reader should feel like they are exploring an interesting idea with the author.
